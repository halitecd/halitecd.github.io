A question worth keeping

A small note on the space between having an impression and making an argument.

Halite·2026.11.01·English
阅读中文版 →

A note does not have to settle a question to be worth keeping. Sometimes its contribution is more modest: it helps us see which question we were asking in the first place.

An impression can contain several unfinished arguments. Writing gives each of them room. A sentence becomes a claim; an example becomes something we can inspect; an uncertainty becomes a place to return to.

The space between evidence and a claim

“Here is what I noticed” and “here is what I think it means” are different sentences. The work between them matters. An example might support a claim without proving that it is always true. A counterexample might narrow a conclusion without making it worthless.

Clear writing can preserve those distinctions. It can offer a judgment and still make its limits visible.

I think this is a useful way to understand the problem. Here is why, and here is where I remain uncertain.

That is a complete beginning, even when it is not a final answer.

A note for a future reader

The future reader may be a friend, a stranger, or the person who wrote the first draft. Give that reader enough context to follow the question. Keep a link to the source. Say what changed between the first impression and the current conclusion.1

The Chinese companion to this sample is 把一个问题写清楚.

An original demonstration text for testing this writing and publishing workflow.

  1. This footnote is an example of a lightweight reference. In a published essay it could hold a book title and page number, or a link to an original source. ↩︎
